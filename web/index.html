1 · The HIMS weekend, replayed from events

Sunday 2026-08-30, Robinhood's busiest day on-chain ($270.6M of stock-token volume, per SQD) and a day with no minting. With nobody able to arbitrage, the cost to push HIMS/USDG 10 % fell from 1,351 USDG at 19:40 UTC to 12 USDG at 23:53 UTC, by when the pool already read 43.27 against a 28.84 NYSE close. The minute close peaked at 71.23 at 23:31; the pool still stood at 54.50 when the first mint landed (00:43), and fell back to 29.31 after it. The largest settlement the pool could safely carry fell from 7,259 USDG at 19:40 to 115 at 23:53 (20 at its minute-level low, 23:25: see the replay): SafeSettle would have refused anything above that. Positions rebuilt from every ModifyLiquidity log; at each point the rebuilt liquidity equals the last Swap event's own liquidity field.

Max safe exposure here is SafeSettle's ladder alone, with nobody pushing back, rounded to the nearest USDG (SafeSettle settles only below the exact figure). At Mon 01:59 the replayed hook's 30-minute TWAPs were 3,396 ticks apart, and pricing that gap SafeSettle v1 would have decided on 2,915 USDG (hims-hook-replay.json, replayBlocks[3].hookPrimary).

2 · Was HIMS a one-off? Every stock pool, one weekend

The deepest USDG pool of each Robinhood stock token, rebuilt from logs from Friday's US close to Monday, priced as the largest settlement it could safely carry with nobody pushing back. On the weekend of 2026-09-18 nothing collapsed: for the 12 pools with a Friday point (MSTR has none), the weekend low was 0.69× to 2.4× of Friday (HIMS 1.06×). On 2026-08-30, by the same measure on a minute grid, HIMS fell to 0.006×: from 3,533 USDG at Friday's close to 20 at Sunday 23:25 (replay). A closed mint window makes a collapse possible, not certain, which is why the bound is measured at settlement.

3 · Measure any pool, right now

Any v4 pool on the official PoolManager, read-only against Robinhood Chain mainnet 4663. The page simulates PushCostLens's deployment, injects its bytecode at a throwaway address with an eth_call state override, and pushes the live pool there and back inside that call: no wallet, no transaction, nothing deployed. It also walks the pool's ticks the way SafeSettle does and computes the largest settlement the pool can safely carry, the number SafeSettle decides on.

4 · Cost to push: a snapshot

Cost = what an attacker loses pushing the price by X and selling straight back (fees on both legs). Capital = the input the push needs, returned afterwards. In USDG.

5 · SafeSettle's decisions

Each row is one settlement from the test suite. SafeSettle prices faking a move of 0.5–20 % (and the gap between the two TWAPs, if any) from the pool's price now, both ways, over the window, re-pushing after every pull-back when arbitrage is open; the smallest cost ÷ gain is the max safe exposure. settle on raw when the total exposure is below it, refuse otherwise. Agreeing TWAPs are not evidence: a push held long enough makes them agree. Figures are rounded to the nearest unit (to 0.01 below 100, two significant digits below 0.01), not down; the exact values are in web/decisions/*.json.
