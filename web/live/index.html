What you are looking at

Max safe exposure is CostModel.maxSafeExposure with arbReversionSeconds = 0: for moves of 0.5–20 % both ways, what pushing the pool there and selling straight back costs in fees, divided by what that move earns per USDG settling on the price. Above it, faking the price pays for itself. The page gets it the way SafeSettle does: it simulates PushCostLens's deployment, injects the bytecode at a throwaway address with an eth_call state override, and runs roundTripCosts both ways against Robinhood Chain mainnet 4663. No wallet, no transaction, nothing deployed.

Friday's close (20:00 UTC, 16:00 ET) and the hourly line come from npm run live:baseline, which rebuilds each pool from its ModifyLiquidity logs, because the public RPC keeps logs but not old state. The two methods agree: at one recent block the script ran both, and the table below shows the ratio.

A falling ratio means the pool got cheaper to fake relative to Friday, usually because liquidity left or the price walked out of the LPs' ranges. It does not say anyone is faking it. A rising ratio can mean an LP added depth, or that the price moved into a thicker part of the book.
