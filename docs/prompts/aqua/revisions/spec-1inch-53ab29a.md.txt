Aqua and SwapVM parts.

## 1. The sophisticated position

One wallet, one balance:

- the wheel: cash-secured puts in USDG and covered calls in TSLA;
- a SwapVM spot market on TSLA/USDG;
- a custom instruction so the two never promise the same tokens.

Collateral leaves the wallet only when an option is bought. Unexercised collateral and premiums come back into the same
Aqua strategy.

## 2. Why Aqua is the right primitive

| Need | Aqua gives |
|---|---|
| The writer keeps custody until a trade | Tokens stay in the maker's wallet; Aqua pulls with `transferFrom` at fill |
| One balance backs several strategies | Virtual balances per `(maker, app, strategyHash, token)` |
| A book can take collateral inside the buy transaction | An **app** contract can `pull` from the maker directly to any recipient |
| Collateral and premium return to where they came from | `push` pays the maker's wallet and credits the strategy |

## 3. Contracts

### 3.1 How Aqua is used (verify against the pinned source)

These names come from `github.com/1inch/aqua` (main `ef24220`) as read on 2026-09-25. **Pin a commit at H0 and check
every name below against it.**

- The maker approves Aqua once per token.
- `ship(...)` records virtual balances for `(maker, app, strategyHash, token)`.
  - No token moves.
  - Aqua does not check the wallet's real balance, so one balance can be promised to many strategies.
- `pull(...)` is called by the **app**. It lowers the virtual balance and `transferFrom`s the maker's wallet to a
  recipient. If the wallet is short, it reverts: first come, first served.
- `push(...)` pays tokens into the maker's wallet and credits the virtual balance.
- `dock(...)` zeroes a strategy.
- `safeBalances(...)` reads virtual balances.
- Events: `Shipped`, `Pulled`, `Pushed`, `Docked`.

Canonical Aqua `0x1111113ccf1426a8e30e2bff5e005d929bf6a90a` is live on Robinhood Chain **4663** and absent from 46630.
The demo runs on a local fork of 4663.

### 3.2 `AquaWriter` (MIT): the Aqua app that writes options

`AquaWriter` plays two roles: it is the **Aqua app** in a maker's strategy, and it implements `ICollateralSource` for
`MiniBook`. The simplest shape is one `AquaWriter` per maker, owned by that maker.

| Function | Behaviour |
|---|---|
| `post(seriesId, premiumPerContract, maxContracts)` | Owner only. Posts a sourced order on `MiniBook` with this contract as maker, and records the promise. |
| `available(seriesId, base, collateral) view` | Returns the smallest of: the Aqua virtual balance for (maker, this, strategyHash, collateral); `IERC20(collateral).balanceOf(maker)`; and the maker's allowance to Aqua. Must stay cheap. It is one figure for all of the writer's open orders: fills are first come, first served, and the book re-reads it at every buy. |
| `provide(seriesId, base, collateral, amount)` | Book only. `AQUA.pull(maker, strategyHash, collateral, amount, book)`: tokens go straight from the maker's wallet to the book. |
| `onReturned(seriesId, base, collateral, amount)` | Book only. The book has sent unexercised escrow here; `AQUA.push(...)` it back into the maker's strategy, so it lands in the wallet and is promised again. |
| `claimPremium(orderId)` | Takes the order's premium from the book and pushes it into the strategy's quote balance. |
| `promised(token) view` | The collateral that open, unfilled orders still promise. Grows at `post`, shrinks at `provide` (the promise became escrow) and at `release`. Read by ExposureGuard. |
| `release(orderId)` | Drops the unfilled part of an order and its promise. Owner any time; anyone once the series expired. |
| `optionDelta() view` | The delta the open option book added to the wallet: −0.5 per short call, +0.5 per short put (at-the-money approximation, WAD in whole base tokens). Read by DeltaSkew. `expire(seriesId)` drops a series once it has expired. |

`AquaWriter` calls Aqua through its published ABI only (`ship` happens from the maker's wallet) and does not derive from
`AquaApp`, so it is MIT and the Aqua license's pure-caller clause applies. `post` refuses a series whose tokens are not the
bound pair, so premiums can never arrive in a token the strategy does not hold.

Invariants to test:

1. Shipping strategies does not change the maker's wallet balance.
2. A fill moves exactly the collateral for `n` contracts, from the maker's wallet, in the same transaction as the buy.
3. `available` never overstates what `provide` can deliver.
4. Unexercised collateral returns to the wallet and is promised again.
5. Premiums end up credited to the maker's strategy, once the series is settled.

### 3.3 `ExposureGuard` (custom SwapVM instruction)

License: `LicenseRef-Degensoft-SwapVM-1.1`; the files live in `contracts/swapvm/`.

- **Router.** Subclass the SwapVM router and register the instruction in a free opcode slot.
  - The router deployed on 4663 (`0x111111338c5091e8440b67b168bae16a668ac0de`) is **v1.0.2**, whose swap is
    `swap(order, tokenIn, tokenOut, amount, takerData)`. Main has changed that signature, so build against tag `v1.0.2`
    throughout.
  - Redeploying a modified router is allowed by the prize rules. Canonical Aqua is used unmodified.
- **Behaviour.** Before the curve instruction runs, compute `free = walletBalance(tokenOut) − AquaWriter.promised(tokenOut)`.
  If the curve's `balanceOut` exceeds `free`, shrink **both** reserves in the same ratio: `balanceOut = free`,
  `balanceIn = ceil(balanceIn × free / balanceOut)`. The marginal price of the curve is unchanged; only its depth is,
  so no fill can ever take more than `free`. At `free == 0` both reserves are zero and the curve refuses to quote.
  Only the token that would leave the maker is looked at, so the other side of the pair is untouched.
- **Why depth and not just a cap on `balanceOut`.** Capping one reserve alone would move the price (with 16,000 USDG
  against 20 instead of 40 TSLA, the curve asks 800 per TSLA). Scaling both keeps the quote honest and simply stops
  earlier.
- **Two forms, one function.** `ExposureGuard` is a native instruction in the subclassed router (opcode 34).
  `ExposureGuardExtruction` wraps the same `capToFree` as an `Extruction` target (opcode 32 of the deployed table), so
  the policy also runs on the canonical router at `0x111111338c…` without redeploying anything. A test quotes both on
  the same state and asserts they agree.
- **Honest framing.** This is a policy: options first. Without it, a spot fill can take tokens that an open option order
  still advertises. A book that re-reads `available` would refuse the option fill (`SourceShort`), so nothing breaks,
  but the depth the buyer saw was not real. `test_WithoutTheGuardASpotFillTakesWhatTheOptionsPromised` shows exactly
  that. The guard reads the whole wallet; it protects the option promises, not the maker's other unguarded strategies
  from each other.
- **`DeltaSkew`.** After the curve, lean the price against the delta the option book added: −0.5 per short call, +0.5
  per short put, at-the-money approximation. Negative delta (the options left the wallet shorter of the base) makes the
  strategy pay more when a taker sells it the base and give less when a taker buys the base; positive delta the
  opposite. `skewBps = min(maxSkewBps, maxSkewBps × |delta| / fullSkewAt)`, both parameters set by the maker in the
  program. This is the delta of the option legs alone, on purpose: a wheel writer keeps its stock and cash exposure
  and only leans against what the options changed. Anyone can move the delta by buying an option, so the maker bounds
  the effect with `maxSkewBps` and the depth it ships. Opcode 35 in the subclassed router.
- **Reference price.** SwapVM's `OraclePriceAdjuster` can peg the spot curve to the same Chainlink TSLA feed that
  `ChainlinkExpiryPrice` uses. Not used in the demo.

## 6a. Settlement trust and liveness

- `ChainlinkExpiryPrice.setFeed` binds a pair to a feed **once**; there is no way to change it afterwards, and the demo
  deploys it from an operator account, not from the maker. `MiniBook.createSeries` refuses a pair the price source does
  not `covers`, and refuses to create a series unless the book's `SETTLE_GRACE` is at least the source's `maxDelay`.
- The reference for an expiry `at` is found in two steps. R is the last round at or before `at`, searched from the
  latest round backwards in id order (down the current phase, then from the last round of the previous phase, and so
  on); rounds of an older phase published after a newer phase started are never R, and both steps are evaluated
  against the proxy's phases as they are at settlement time. If R is within `maxAge` before `at`, R is the reference.
  Otherwise F, the round right after R (the earlier of the next id in R's phase and the first round of the next
  phase), is the reference if it is within `maxAge` after `at`; that covers a feed that was quiet before expiry on a
  24/5 market. Only when neither qualifies does settlement revert with `StaleRound`.
- `priceAt(base, quote, at)` walks back from the latest round for at most 256 steps. `priceAt(base, quote, at,
  abi.encode(uint80 roundId))` takes R from the caller, verifies it in a bounded number of calls (published at or
  before `at`, and the round right after it, skipping ids without data and empty phases, published after `at`), and
  applies the second step itself; `MiniBook.settle(seriesId, hint)` passes it through. `hintFor` is a view running the
  same bounded walk; for an expiry further back than 256 rounds an indexer reads R from the feed's history. A fuzz over
  random three-phase layouts (ties, gaps, empty phases, overlaps) tries every existing round as a hint and finds
  exactly the walk's R accepted, with the walk's price.
- Premiums stay in the book until the series is settled; `claimPremium` then pays the maker, so a writer has to see
  its series settled (anyone can call `settle`) to be paid. If nobody settled within `SETTLE_GRACE` of expiry, for
  whatever reason, the series can no longer be settled: makers `close` and get the whole escrow back, and holders
  `refund` their contracts for a pro-rata-by-contract share of the premiums paid on the series. Refunds depend on
  time alone, never on any maker's `close`, so no maker can hold another maker's buyers hostage. `SETTLE_GRACE` must
  exceed the source's `maxDelay`, so a first round after expiry always leaves time to settle.
- The exercise window counts from the moment of settlement, not from expiry, so a late settlement never silently
  strips holders of their window. `close` opens only after the window. `settle` and the unsettled `close` are
  mutually exclusive in time (at most `expiry + SETTLE_GRACE` versus strictly after it).
- Exercise uses strict moneyness (at-the-money contracts are not exercisable).
- If a maker `dock`s the strategy its `AquaWriter` is bound to, `available` becomes zero and a `close` (or a premium
  claim) cannot push into a docked strategy, so it reverts. The maker ships a fresh strategy and calls `bind` again;
  the return then lands there. Only the maker's own escrow and premium are affected; holders' exercise and refunds
  do not depend on it.
- Known limits: the reference depends on the proxy's phase state at settlement time, and the search assumes phases
  start in id order (the normal Chainlink upgrade path). A book on a feed with a different upgrade history should
  settle promptly after expiry.
